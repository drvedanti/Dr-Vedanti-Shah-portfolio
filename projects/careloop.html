01 · Healthcare Product · UX · Build

CareLoop

Keep care moving. Together.

A care-coordination product exploring how families can manage an elderly parent's care journey as a connected system of events, people, responsibilities and dependencies.

Role Product thinking · UX · Build
Domain Healthcare / Care coordination
Status Working product prototype

01 · The problem

Care doesn't happen one task at a time.

When an elderly parent needs ongoing care, information can move between doctors, family members, appointments, reports, instructions and everyday responsibilities.

A blood test isn't simply a blood test. It may lead to a report, which may affect a follow-up appointment. The person responsible for one step may also change.

CareLoop started from the idea that a care product should preserve those relationships instead of treating every event as an isolated reminder.

02 · Product hypothesis

Healthcare journeys are stateful, but healthcare software is often transactional.

Instead of building another reminder list, I explored a journey-based model where events, people, dependencies and changing states are represented together.

Blood test → Medical report → Follow-up

03 · Product model

The journey became the core product object.

CareLoop represents more than a list of tasks. A journey contains the patient, people involved, care events, dependencies, responsibilities, current state and activity history.

01

Events

What needs to happen — from consultations and tests to everyday care tasks.

02

Dependencies

What affects what comes next, such as a report depending on a completed test.

03

People

Who is involved and who is responsible for each part of the journey.

04

State

Whether an event is pending, complete or needs attention.

04 · From information to structure

Turning caregiver language into something the product can work with.

Care information often begins as natural language — messages, instructions and notes. CareLoop's extraction layer identifies structured events, timing, people, dependencies and information that may need clarification.

Implementation evidence

  • Care events are represented as structured objects.
  • Timing and caregiver assignment are preserved.
  • Dependencies can connect one care event to another.
  • Missing timing can generate clarification questions.

05 · Designing for change

What happens when the person responsible can no longer do the task?

CareLoop models responsibility as something that can change, rather than making the person permanently part of the task.

Assigned → Unavailable → Needs attention → Reassigned

The underlying care event remains part of the journey while responsibility can move between members of the care circle.

06 · Care Circle

Care coordination is shared responsibility.

CareLoop treats the care circle as a first-class part of the product. Members can have roles and contact information, and the product supports an invitation lifecycle connected to a specific journey.

Not invited → Invited → Accepted

07 · Building the system

From a visual concept to a working product.

The product evolved through iterative implementation rather than stopping at the interface layer.

01

Application state

The journey maintains events, people, dependencies, reminders and changing status.

02

Persistence

Journey state can be saved and retrieved through a Supabase-backed server layer.

03

Care circle data

Family members and invitation states are persisted separately from the journey state.

04

Deployment

The product is deployed and has gone through iterative development and testing.

09 · What I learned

Building the product changed how I think about the problem.

A healthcare workflow is not necessarily a collection of independent tasks. Relationships between events can themselves become part of the product.

The project also pushed me beyond interface design into state modelling, persistence, edge cases, responsibility changes and the technical consequences of product decisions.

10 · What remains unvalidated

A working prototype is not the same as product-market validation.

CareLoop is currently a working product prototype, not a clinically validated or commercially deployed product.

The next stage would be testing the underlying assumptions with real caregivers: whether families think in terms of a shared journey, which dependencies matter most, where automation helps, and what information people are comfortable sharing.

11 · The next question

Can a shared, stateful care journey make family coordination easier without creating another system families have to maintain?
