KAALJI

Gap in care provision after caregiver training

Independent research & product concept India Exploratory secondary research

01 · The question

What happens after a caregiver is trained?

India has been making significant efforts to train, register and organise caregivers for older adults. While looking into these efforts, one question kept coming up: what happens after a caregiver is trained?

I do not live with my parents, and as they get older, I think about what would happen if they needed regular support and I could not be physically present.

There are agencies, geriatric-care services and other options available, but knowing that options exist does not automatically make them reassuring. Is the caregiver properly trained? Are they verified? Are they reliable? And if something goes wrong, who is accountable?

That made me curious about how India is preparing for an ageing population and what kind of elder-care system is already being built.

02 · Research

I first assumed the problem might simply be a lack of caregivers.

I began by reviewing government programmes, caregiver-training initiatives, elder-care services, startups, and published data on ageing and functional support. This led me to PM-SPECIAL, SHATAYU, JEEVAN and the Longitudinal Ageing Study in India (LASI).

Before accepting the shortage assumption, I wanted to understand how large the care need actually was and what support already existed.

I did not interpret the LASI figures as proof of demand for paid caregivers. They show that functional support needs exist, but they do not tell us who would require paid care, who would rely on family, or what kind of support would eventually be needed.

SHATAYU and related initiatives also showed that work was being done around caregiver registration and discoverability. So I knew that training was happening and that systems were being created to make trained caregivers easier to identify.

03 · The gap

The part I could not follow clearly was the transition from training to actual care.

I could find how many people had been trained. I could find information on registration. I could find evidence of functional support needs among older adults.

I could not find a comparable national view of how many trained caregivers entered the workforce, remained active, were available to families, delivered care or stayed in caregiving over time.

Instead of focusing only on whether more caregivers should be trained, I began asking: what happens after training?
Trained→Registered→Active→Available→Providing care→Retained

This is a proposed framework, not a measured national conversion funnel. The available evidence did not allow me to calculate conversion rates across every stage.

A trained caregiver is not automatically an active caregiver. Registration may make someone easier to find, but it does not tell us whether that person is currently working, available in a particular location, providing care, or remaining in the profession.

What the research supports

  • Functional support needs exist among older adults.
  • Formal caregiver training is taking place.
  • Registration and discoverability systems are being developed.

What it does not establish

  • A national caregiver shortage.
  • A national training-to-employment conversion rate.
  • The number of currently active trained caregivers.
  • The proportion of trained capacity that becomes care delivered.

04 · KAALJI

A concept for looking at the caregiver journey after training.

I did not want to create another caregiver marketplace or directory because different versions of those already exist through private companies, NGOs and other elder-care services.

I was more interested in the journey itself.

  • How many trained caregivers are actually working?
  • Are registered caregivers active?
  • Where are caregivers available?
  • Can families find someone in the location where they need support?
  • Is training and registration resulting in more care being delivered?
  • Are caregivers staying in the workforce?

KAALJI is intended to bring these questions together and make gaps in the journey easier to see.

At this stage, it is only a concept. The next step would be primary research with caregiver-training programmes, elder-care organisations and workforce managers to understand what they currently track, where their information becomes incomplete, and whether a broader view of the caregiver journey would help them make better decisions.

05 · Why this project

I started by asking whether I could trust the care system if my own parents needed support.

The research took me somewhere different.

I could not prove that India simply needs more caregivers. What I did find was that the later stages of the caregiver journey are much harder to follow than training and registration.

KAALJI came from wanting to understand that missing part of the system.
