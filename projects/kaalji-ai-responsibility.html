Where AI belongs — and where it should stop

KAALJI does not need AI to make the final decision. The useful role for AI is narrower: help people find, understand and explore evidence faster, while important checks and decisions stay visible.

My design rule:
AI can make evidence easier to navigate. It should not decide whether unlike datasets are comparable, invent missing evidence, or own consequential decisions.
1

AI helps with language and exploration

Useful for language, search and exploration
Find relevant information

Help locate sources or passages related to the user's question.

Explain in plain language

Summarize what a source says without changing what it means.

Surface possible explanations

Suggest hypotheses worth investigating — clearly labelled as possibilities, not facts.

Help someone ask a better question

Turn a vague concern into a clearer evidence question.

→
2

Evidence rules stay explicit

Important checks should be reproducible and open to inspection
Can the numbers be compared?

Check geography, time period, population, definition and what each number measures.

Keep the source attached

Every important figure should remain connected to where it came from.

Do the calculation visibly

Derived numbers should come from clear formulas rather than hidden AI arithmetic.

Keep labels intact

Fact, calculation, hypothesis and missing data should not silently turn into one another.

→
3

People own the decision

Consequential judgment and accountability stay with the user
What does the evidence mean here?

Context still matters. A system cannot understand every local care reality from a spreadsheet.

What should be investigated next?

KAALJI can suggest a direction, but the user decides what is worth pursuing.

What action should be taken?

Programme, policy, funding and healthcare decisions should remain accountable to people.

When is the evidence not good enough?

A person must be able to say: “We do not know enough yet.”

Example

Question: “Does this training number prove we have enough caregivers?”

AI can help

Find the training source and explain what the reported figure refers to.

Rules check

Training is a pipeline measure. Without employment and availability data, it cannot be treated as active workforce supply.

Human judgment

Whether the next step should be employment data, local availability research, affordability research, or something else.

KAALJI should not let AI…

  • invent a missing number;
  • treat two incompatible datasets as comparable;
  • turn functional care need into paid-care demand;
  • declare a caregiver shortage without supporting evidence;
  • upgrade a hypothesis into a fact;
  • make autonomous healthcare, programme or policy decisions.

Why I draw the boundary here

  • AI is useful when the information is large and fragmented.
  • It is less trustworthy when a precise answer requires missing context.
  • Visible rules make the product easier to question and audit.
  • Human ownership keeps responsibility clear when decisions matter.
