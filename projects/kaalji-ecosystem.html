India 2050 · Product Landscape

KAALJI is not another way to book a caregiver

One of the most important product decisions in this project came from looking at what was already being built. India already has government tools supporting senior citizens and caregiver visibility, while private providers arrange and deliver care at home. That changed the question from “How can people find care?” to “How can decision-makers understand what is happening across the care system?”

The product shift: I originally considered a caregiver-discovery idea. SHATAYU and the existing home-care market made that less interesting. The gap I kept running into during the research was different: the evidence for need, training, registration, employment, availability, affordability and use sits in different places and does not automatically tell one coherent story.
Government support

JEEVAN

A government mobile application for senior citizens, designed around areas such as welfare information, emergency support, healthcare access and social inclusion.

Main job

Help senior citizens access information and support.

Caregiver visibility

SHATAYU

A government dashboard where trained geriatric caregivers can register, with information on caregiver availability by district and state.

Main job

Make trained caregivers more visible and discoverable.

Private care delivery

Home-care providers

Providers such as Portea and Care24 help families arrange services including caregivers, attendants, nursing and other forms of care at home.

Main job

Arrange and deliver care to families.

Proposed decision support

KAALJI

A proposed evidence and decision-support layer for understanding what the care-system data can tell us, what it cannot tell us, and what should be investigated next.

Main job

Help planners and programme teams understand the system before choosing an intervention.

The gap KAALJI is exploring

A training programme can tell us how many people entered training. A registration system can make caregivers visible. A home-care company can deliver services to its customers. But a planner may still need to understand why a region appears to have weak care access — and whether the issue is training, employment, visibility, affordability, utilization, retention, or simply missing evidence.

KAALJI's question:
“Before choosing the solution, do we understand what kind of problem this actually is?”

What I am not claiming

  • KAALJI has validated product-market fit.
  • KAALJI is superior to the tools or providers shown here.
  • Existing services cover every geography or care need.
  • SHATAYU registration equals active employment or delivered care.
  • This landscape is an exhaustive map of India's elder-care market.
